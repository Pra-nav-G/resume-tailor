# User Interview Guide

The talk is clear that the model is your first interviewer, not your last: it means you walk into real conversations with sharper questions. This guide is for those conversations.
Log every interview in `marketing-doc.md` §7b, then update the gate answers it changes.

## Before: pick what you're testing

1. Take the top 3 rows of your ranked assumptions (`marketing-doc.md` §7). Those are what the interviews must test, not "do people like my idea."
2. Write down, before you talk to anyone, what answer would count as evidence against each one.
3. Find 5 people who match your ideal first customer (Gate 3). Five conversations show patterns; one shows an opinion.

To turn your assumptions into a script, use the prompt in `playbook/prompts.md` → Interview script.

## The rules

- **Talk about their life, not your idea.** Don't pitch until the end, if at all. Once they know what you're building, they'll try to be nice about it.
- **Ask about the past, not the future.** "When did that last happen?" gets facts. "Would you use…?" gets guesses.
- **Ask for specifics.** Times, tools, costs, who else was involved.
- **Listen more than you talk.** Aim for them talking 80% of the time.
- **Look for what they already do and pay for.** A workaround they built themselves is the strongest signal there is.

## Questions that work

**The problem (Gate 1)**
- Tell me about the last time you dealt with [problem]. What happened?
- How are you handling it today?
- What does that cost you, in time, money, or hassle?
- How often does it come up?
- What have you tried that didn't work? Why did you stop?

**The market and alternatives (Gate 2)**
- What else have you looked at? What did you like or hate about it?
- If you could wave a wand and change one thing about how you do this, what would it be?

**Money and decisions (Gate 4)**
- Who decides whether to spend money on something like this? Who else gets a say?
- What do you pay for today that's closest to this?
- How did you choose what you're using now?

**Wrapping up**
- Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?
- Who else should I talk to about this?
- Can I follow up when I have something to show you?

## Questions to avoid

| Avoid | Why | Ask instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Would you use an app that…?" | Hypothetical; people say yes to be kind | "How do you handle that today?" |
| "Do you think this is a good idea?" | Asks for an opinion, gets a compliment | "When did this last cost you something?" |
| "How much would you pay for this?" | Future guess, almost always too high | "What do you pay for today that's closest?" |
| "Don't you hate it when…?" | Leading; puts the answer in their mouth | "Tell me about the last time…" |

## Red flags in what you hear

- **Compliments:** "That's really cool!" is not data.
- **Future promises:** "I'd definitely use that" or "I'd probably pay". Count only past behaviour and commitments.
- **Generic answers:** "I usually…", "I always…". Ask for the last specific time.
- **No workaround:** if they've never tried to solve it, it may not hurt enough (vitamin, not painkiller).

## Signals that count

- They describe the problem in the same words you used, without prompting.
- They've built their own workaround or are paying for a bad solution.
- They give you something: time for a follow-up, an introduction, a pre-order, or access to their data.

## After each interview

1. Within an hour, write it up in `marketing-doc.md` §7b: who, what they said (quotes), and which assumption it supports or weakens.
2. After every 5 interviews, update the gate answers and assumption ranking. If a kill criterion was hit, say so out loud.
3. Bring the notes back to Claude Chat: "Here are my interview notes. Which assumptions do they support, which do they weaken, and what should I change in marketing-doc.md?"

Further reading: *The Mom Test* by Rob Fitzpatrick is the standard short book on this kind of interviewing.
